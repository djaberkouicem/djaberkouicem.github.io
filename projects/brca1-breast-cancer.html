BRCA1 & Breast Cancer

← Back to Projects

Project Type
Computational Genetics / Structural Bioinformatics
Focus
BRCA1 · RING Domain · C61G · Breast Cancer
Tools
R · UniProt · RCSB PDB · PyMOL
Research Question How can disease-associated BRCA1 RING-domain variants be characterized using sequence information and structural visualization? More specifically — where does C61G occur within the BRCA1 RING-domain structure, and what structural consequences might this substitution have?

Why BRCA1?

BRCA1 is a tumor suppressor gene strongly linked to hereditary breast and ovarian cancer. Its protein product participates in DNA damage repair and cell-cycle regulation, and pathogenic variants significantly increase lifetime cancer risk.

Why the RING Domain?

The N-terminal region of BRCA1 (approximately residues 1–100) forms a RING domain that heterodimerizes with BARD1 and contributes to the E3 ubiquitin ligase activity of the BRCA1–BARD1 complex. This region is not defined by a single exact boundary in the literature; it is best understood through its structural elements — including two zinc-binding sites formed by residues such as C24, C27, C39, H41, C44, C47, C61, and C64 — rather than as a fixed 1–100 cutoff.

Methods

The workflow followed these steps:

  1. Protein sequence obtained from UniProt (P38398).
  2. Variant information cross-checked against published functional and clinical literature.
  3. Wild-type and variant amino-acid sequences compared programmatically in R.
  4. Variant positions examined within the BRCA1 RING-domain structural context.
  5. Structural information obtained from RCSB PDB entry 1JM7.
  6. The structure was visualized and inspected using PyMOL.
  7. Variants were interpreted within their structural context.

(The complete R scripts and raw/intermediate files are kept private; this page summarizes methodology and results rather than publishing the full implementation.)

Main Case Study: C61G

The sequence comparison in R was used to identify the exact amino-acid difference between the reference and variant sequences, verify the mutation position, and establish the correspondence between wild-type and variant residues: 

ref_letters[61]   # "C"
mut_letters[61]   # "G"

This confirmed a cysteine-to-glycine substitution at position 61 (C61G).

Comparative RING-Domain Variants

To place C61G in context, it is compared below with two other documented RING-domain substitutions affecting the same zinc-coordinating residue set:

Variant AA Change Position Evidence / Annotation Structural Context
C61G Cys → Gly 61 Cancer-predisposing; abolished E3 ubiquitin ligase activity in vitro (Brzovic et al., 2001, PNAS) Zinc-coordinating residue, RING domain
C64Y Cys → Tyr 64 Cancer-predisposing; abolished E3 ubiquitin ligase activity in vitro (Brzovic et al., 2001, PNAS) Zinc-coordinating residue, RING domain
C64G Cys → Gly 64 Reported to disrupt RING-domain structural integrity (literature review, secondary source) Zinc-coordinating residue, RING domain

This table compiles findings reported in the cited literature; it is not a clinical classification and was not generated by independent experimental testing in this project.

Structural Visualization

Results

  • C61G corresponds to a cysteine-to-glycine substitution at residue 61 of BRCA1.
  • Residue 61 lies within the RING-domain structural context and participates in a zinc-coordinating environment, as shown in PDB 1JM7.
  • Two additional documented substitutions at nearby zinc-coordinating residues (C64Y, C64G) were identified from the literature for comparison.
  • Structural visualization provides a way to generate hypotheses about the potential effects of these substitutions, based on their location relative to the zinc-binding sites.

Interpretation

Observed

Position 61 is located within the RING-domain structural context, and Cys61 participates in the zinc-coordinating environment shown in the PDB 1JM7 model.

Interpretation / Hypothesis

Because Cys61 contributes to this zinc-coordinating environment, replacing cysteine with glycine is expected to alter the local structural environment. The structural visualization provides a mechanistic hypothesis for how C61G may affect RING-domain integrity.

Not directly established by this project

The exact functional consequences of C61G (e.g., degree of loss of ubiquitin ligase activity) are not measured here; that evidence comes from external functional studies (cited above), not from this visualization alone.

Limitations

  • The structural model provides spatial context but does not experimentally measure the functional effect of C61G.
  • A substitution shown in a structural model does not by itself demonstrate loss of activity.
  • PDB 1JM7 is an NMR solution structure and represents a structural model rather than a direct experimental measurement of the C61G variant specifically.
  • Additional computational or experimental approaches would be required to quantify the effect of individual variants.

Conclusion

This project demonstrates how sequence analysis and structural visualization can be combined to investigate disease-associated BRCA1 variants. C61G provides a useful case study because the substitution occurs within the zinc-coordinating RING-domain environment. The analysis supports a structural hypothesis for how the substitution may alter the local environment, while recognizing that functional consequences require additional evidence beyond structural visualization alone.
